Nancy: DEC VAX-11/780

A 1979 minicomputer running 4.3BSD (Berkeley, 1986), emulated in your browser!

Using it

  • Click the screen, then log in as root (no password).
  • Run setenv TERM vt100 before vi or more.
  • Try ls /usr/games, /usr/games/adventure, /usr/games/rogue, man ls.
  • Arrow keys don't recall commands. Use history instead.
  • Ctrl-E pauses the machine and opens the sim> prompt. Type c to resume.

The tty00 and tty01 tabs are more terminals on the same machine. Log in on two of them and try who, or write root tty00 to message another user.

Your disk

Files you create are saved in this browser and are there next time. Before leaving, sync makes sure they've reached the disk.

Reset disk throws your changes away and goes back to a fresh install.

The VAX-11/780

The 780 was the first of DEC's VAX computers. It has a 200-nanosecond clock cycle, an 8 KB memory cache, and a 128-entry translation cache. It runs at about one MIPS, and became the standard that other computers' speeds were measured against.

This emulation has 8 MB of memory and one 456 MB RA81 disk.

Nancy's history

Nancy was the Brown CS department's first general-purpose computer and the first VAX sold in Rhode Island. It was ordered in the spring of 1978 and installed on January 8, 1979, in a borrowed room in Prince Engineering Laboratory. In May 1979 it moved to the basement of Kassar House. It stayed there, though it was repositioned three times during construction of Gould Laboratory, until it was taken out of service at 10:27am on June 17, 1988.

Nancy was the department's only time-sharing machine until Sluggo, another VAX-11/780, joined it on October 6, 1982. It was used for research and departmental administration throughout its life, and for teaching in 1980 and 1981. It first ran DEC's VMS operating system. UNIX was installed in June 1979.

It started with 256 KB of memory and a single 67 MB disk drive. When it was retired, it had 10 MB of memory and almost 2 GB of disk storage.
